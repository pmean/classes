Key Points: Module 12 – Two-Factor Analysis of Variance
Two-Factor ANOVA Fundamentals

Analyzes a continuous outcome with two categorical predictors simultaneously. 1
Balanced data requires proportional observations in each category combination; unbalanced data creates complexity in how averages are computed. 23
Mathematical Model

The model is: Y_ijk = μ + αi + βj + ε_ijk, where αi and βj represent deviations for each factor. 4
Indicator variables are created for each categorical factor (K levels → K−1 indicators). 5
Relationship to Linear Regression

The intercept estimates the average at the two reference (alphabetically first) categories; slopes represent changes when switching categories, holding the other constant. 67
Checking Assumptions

Three assumptions: normality, homogeneity, and independence — same as one-factor ANOVA. 8
No need to check linearity; no non-parametric analog exists for two-factor ANOVA. 910
Log transformation may help when data is right-skewed and larger means have larger variation. 11
Interactions

An interaction occurs when the effect of one variable depends on the level of another. 12
Non-parallel lines in an interaction plot indicate a significant interaction. 13
When interaction is present, effects cannot be described independently — the combined effect must be interpreted. 1415
Synergy = combined effect exceeds sum of parts; antagonism = combined effect is sub-additive. 1617
Interactions cannot be estimated when only one observation exists per cell. 18
Fruit Fly Example

Male fruit flies with 8 virgin females showed a ~26-day decline in lifespan — far greater than the additive effects of crowding (~1.4 days) and mating opportunity (~8 days) alone, confirming a significant interaction. 1920
