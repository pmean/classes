Key Topics Covered in This Lecture (2026-10-27)
1. Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) Overview

ANOVA is a statistical method used to compare means of three or more groups, testing whether at least one group mean differs significantly. 12
Null hypothesis: all group means are equal; Alternative: at least one differs. 3
2. Sum of Squares & Degrees of Freedom

Total variation (SST) partitions into explained variation (SSB) and unexplained variation (SSE/SSW). 45
R² = SSB/SST represents the proportion of explained variation. 4
Degrees of freedom: SST = N−1, SSW = N−K, SSB = K−1. 67
3. F-Test

F = MS Between / MS Within; reject H₀ for large F values or small p-values (< alpha). 89
F can never be negative; F ≈ 1 means group variation is comparable to within-group variation. 1011
4. ANOVA Assumptions

Normality, homogeneity of variance, and independence. 12
Box plots are recommended for checking skewness, outliers, and heterogeneity. 1314
5. Confidence Intervals & Multiple Comparisons

Tukey post hoc test identifies which group pairs differ after rejecting H₀, adjusting for multiple comparisons. 1516
Bonferroni adjustment: test each hypothesis at α/M. 17
Alternatives include Dunnett test (treatment vs. control) and Scheffé test (complex comparisons). 1819

Used aov(), tidy(), and TukeyHSD() functions; box plots and descriptive statistics by depth group. 2021
7. Sample Size Justification

Used power.anova.test() specifying groups, between.var, within.var, sig.level, and power. 22
A sample of 37 per group achieves 90% power; with 10% dropout (n=33), power remains ~87%. 2324
