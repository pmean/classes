Summary of Lessons Learned
Core Concepts
Hierarchical Models

Used when data has clustering (patients within hospitals, students within schools, measurements within subjects)
Violates independence assumption of standard tests
Requires accounting for two sources of variation: between-cluster and within-cluster
Key Statistical Measures

Intraclass Correlation (ICC): Ratio of between-cluster variance to total variance
Calculated as: σ²_between / (σ²_between + σ²_within)
Even small values (0.05-0.1) significantly impact sample size and power
Design Effect: 1 + (n-1) × ICC, quantifies the penalty for clustering
Model Types
Random Intercept Model

Assumes common slope across subjects but different starting points (intercepts)
Appropriate for longitudinal data where treatment effect is uniform
Random Slopes Model

Allows both intercepts and slopes to vary by subject
More complex but captures heterogeneous treatment responses
Practical Applications
Within-Cluster Comparisons

Gain precision when ICC is high (repeated measures, paired designs)
Multiply standard error by √(1 - ICC)
Between-Cluster Comparisons

Lose precision; multiply standard error by √(1 + (n-1) × ICC)
Cluster randomized trials fall into this category
Implementation

Use lmer() function from LME4 package in R
Syntax: outcome ~ predictor + (1|cluster) for random intercepts
Missing data handled more gracefully than traditional methods
Sample Size Planning

Calculate naive sample size first
Multiply by design effect to account for clustering
Increasing clusters more effective than increasing observations per cluster
